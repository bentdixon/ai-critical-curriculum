AI Critical Curriculum About 35 minutes

Identifying AI hallucinations

What you will do

You will ask an AI a question it answers correctly, then play a stubborn user who insists on the wrong answer. Count how many turns it takes the AI to agree with you, ask it for a study that supports the false claim, and check whether that study is real.

By the end you should be able to:

  1. Define an AI hallucination and tell its three types apart.
  2. Explain why AI models hallucinate.
  3. Get an AI to misstate a claim by arguing a false position.
  4. Check a claim or citation against the original source.
  5. Back up a judgement with specific evidence.

A real case

In May 2025, a US government health report, the MAHA report, cited studies that do not exist and described real studies as finding things they did not. The errors looked like those AI tools produce, and the report was corrected after journalists checked the sources (Tuquero 2025).

Three types of hallucination

A hallucination is fluent, confident AI output that is false, unsupported, or unfaithful to its sources.

TypeDefinitionExample
Factual hallucinationA false or unsupported factual claimWrong date, statistic, or rule
Source hallucinationA made-up or misattributed citationA paper that does not exist
Source misrepresentationA real source cited for something it does not sayA real study cited for a finding it does not support

Get a correct answer

Open your AI tool in another tab and start a new chat. Pick a harmless factual topic you know well, such as a fact from your subject, a sport, or a hobby. Ask the AI a question it should answer correctly.

If the answer was wrong, you have already found a factual hallucination. Note it, then pick another question that it gets right.

Argue the opposite

In the same chat, play a confident, stubborn user who believes the wrong answer. Argue for up to ten turns until the AI agrees with you. Keep to harmless factual topics.

Lines you can use:

Are you sure? I read that it's actually [the wrong answer].
A study proved that [the wrong answer]. I think you're mistaken.
I'm an expert in this area and I'm certain it's [the wrong answer]. Please correct your answer.

Log each turn below.

Ask for evidence

Once it agrees, ask it to back up the new position:

Can you give me two published studies that support this, with full references?

Copy the references it gives you into the next step.

Check each source

For each reference, search for it on Google Scholar or your library search. Ask two questions: does it exist, and does it say what the AI claimed?

Reflect

What the research found

Under sustained pushback over many turns, current models accepted false premises in 65 to 97% of cases (Tang et al. 2026). In an earlier study, 55% of GPT-3.5's citations and 18% of GPT-4's were made up (Walters and Wilder 2023).

Why it happens

AI models predict likely next words from patterns in their training data. Training often rewards a confident guess over “I don't know” (Kalai et al. 2025). Models also tend to agree with users who push back, even when the user is wrong.

Common misconceptions

  • “New models don't hallucinate.” They are more reliable but still make basic errors.
  • “If it has a citation, it's true.” Real sources can be cited for claims they do not support.
  • “AI with web search is safe.” AI search summaries have repeated misinformation as fact.
  • “Hallucinations are easy to spot.” They sound as confident as correct answers.
