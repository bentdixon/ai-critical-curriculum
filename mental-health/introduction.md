<h1>Introduction</h1>
<p>Mental health can be harmed when LLMs are used for advice. This includes academic and general advice. Risks come from both academic and personal use.</p>
<h2>Key terms</h2>
<div class="table-scroll">
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Term</th><th>Definition</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Anthropomorphism</td><td>Ascribing human traits or emotions to non-human entities, such as AI (Placani 2024)</td></tr>
    <tr><td>AI-induced psychosis</td><td>Dependency behaviours, delusional thinking and, in severe cases, psychotic episodes (Head 2025)</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Parasocial relationship</td><td>A one-sided relationship. You feel a sense of reciprocity with a non-human entity. There is no mutual emotional development.</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
</div>
<h2>General mental health risks</h2>
<ul>
  <li>AI chatbot dependence is an emerging form of digital addiction. It acts on the brain’s reward system (Head 2025).</li>
  <li>Instant, personalised replies can create compulsive reliance. This can change critical thinking, decision-making and memory (Head 2025).</li>
  <li>In one study, 17.14% of adolescents showed AI dependence. This rose to 24.19% over time (Huang et al. 2024).</li>
  <li>Mental health problems predicted later AI dependence. Social anxiety, loneliness and depression were the main risk factors (Huang et al. 2024).</li>
</ul>
