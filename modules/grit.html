AI Critical Curriculum About 35 minutes

Grit: struggling with hard problems when AI is available

A question to start

Think of the last hard problem you used AI for. Could you solve it on your own now, without looking?

What you will do

You will solve two pairs of short probability and counting problems. In each pair, the second problem uses the same idea as the first.

  • Round 1, AI first. An AI solves the first problem for you. Then you solve the second one alone.
  • Round 2, struggle first. You work on the first problem alone, with hints if you need them. Then you solve the second one alone.

You will need your AI tool in another tab, and paper and a pen.

By the end you should be able to:

  1. Tell the difference between doing well with AI and lasting learning.
  2. Explain why productive struggle improves learning.
  3. Describe how relying on AI for hard problems can build up over time.
  4. Identify which steps of a problem you cannot do without AI.
  5. Set a personal rule for when to struggle before asking AI.

    What the research found

    In a trial with nearly 1,000 high school students, those who practised maths with plain GPT-4 scored 48% higher on practice problems. But when the AI was taken away for the exam, they scored 17% lower than students who had never had it (Bastani et al. 2025).

    Your two rounds are a demonstration, not an experiment. The problems differ in difficulty, and you did them in a fixed order, so treat your own result as something to think about rather than proof.

    Key ideas

    IdeaWhat it means
    Desirable difficultyHarder practice feels slower but is remembered better (Bjork 1994).
    Productive failureStruggling with a problem before being taught how to solve it beats being taught first (Sinha and Kapur 2021).
    Skill decaySkills you do not use fade, and thinking skills fade fastest (Arthur et al. 1998).

    Why it builds up

    Confidence grows from doing hard things yourself (Bandura 1977), and people with less confidence hand more of their thinking to AI (Lee et al. 2025). Each hard problem you give to AI skips both the practice and the success, so the next hard problem feels harder, and you are more likely to hand that one over too.

    Common misconceptions

    • “If I got the right answer with AI, I learned it.” Doing well with a tool is not the same as learning.
    • “Struggling means I'm doing it wrong.” Struggle is often a sign that learning is happening.
    • “AI always harms learning.” Well-designed AI tutors can help (Kestin et al. 2025). How you use it matters.

    Set a rule for yourself

    Write a rule for when you will struggle on your own before asking AI. For example: “15 minutes alone, then hints only, never the full answer.”
